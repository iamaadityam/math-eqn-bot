Help — how the equations work

The goal

Your soldier (orange) stands somewhere on the map. Type an equation; when you press Fire! the graph of that equation is drawn starting at your soldier, travelling to the right. Every enemy (blue) the curve touches is killed. Kill all enemies to win.

The map

A normal Cartesian plane: x from −25 to 25 (left → right), y from −15 to 15 (bottom → top). The thick lines are the axes and each small square is 1 unit. Dark blobs are obstacles: if your curve touches one the shot ends there and blows a hole in it. Green soldiers are your allies — your shots kill them too.

How your equation becomes a shot

The curve is always shifted up or down so that it passes through your soldier. That shift is the number shown at the right of the input (e.g. + 6.137). So only the shape of your function matters — not where it would normally cross the axes.

Important: x is the real x‑coordinate on the map, not the distance from you. If you stand at x = −20, then x^2 already has a steep slope at your feet. To get shapes centred on yourself, use (x+20) (if you stand at −20) instead of x.

Tip: to make a curve hit several points, add terms that vanish at the points you already have, e.g. (x−a)(x−b) is 0 at both a and b.

Special zones

Shield
red dashed circle		 Around a shielded enemy. The first time your curve touches it, your curve is replaced by its derivative f′(x) — the slope function of what you typed — lifted so it continues from the contact point (no jump). A parabola becomes a line, a sine becomes a cosine, a straight line becomes flat. Works in every mode (in the differential modes the "curve" is the integrated path). Each shield triggers once per shot; the shield disappears when its enemy dies.
Parry bot
purple circle		 A bot that doesn't attack — it defends. Its parry field sits in front of it. Anything that enters is bent back toward you, and your own shot can kill you or your allies. Go around the field (over or under) to reach the bot behind it. Kill the bot and the field vanishes.
Assist field
green dashed circle		 Every ally deploys one (there's no limit on how many allies you can have). If your shot enters it, the shot is caught and steered by the equation you type in that ally's field box: inside the field x = distance travelled in it, y = heading angle in radians (0 = right, + = up), and the value you give is the turn rate (+ turns left/up, − right/down). 0.7 = steady left curl, sin(x*3) = wiggle, 2*(1.57−y) = swing toward straight up. After the shot leaves a field it flies straight along its exit heading, so it can be caught by another field — chain fields to bounce one shot through many enemies. Each field catches a shot once. Empty equation = no steering.
A caught shot is no longer a function of x: it can go backwards or down. A shot redirected by any field can hit you.

What ends a shot

  • The curve leaves the map.
  • It touches an obstacle (the obstacle gets a crater).
  • The function becomes undefined — e.g. ln(−1), 1/0 or sqrt of a negative.
Soldiers do not stop a shot — it carries on and can kill several in one go (allies included). If a field sends your shot back into you, you lose that round.

Function types

f(x)The default. The graph of y = f(x), shifted to start at you.
y' = f(x, y)You give the slope of the path at every point; the game integrates it from your position. y' = 0.1 is a line with slope 0.1; y' = -y/10 bends the path back toward the x‑axis. You can use x and y (the height of the path right now).
y'' = f(x, y, y')You give the curvature. You also set a starting angle (degrees, 0 = flat, positive = up). y'' = -0.05 with angle 30° fires a parabolic arc. You can use x, y and y'.
